Statutory Legal Foundation & Governing Act

In Alberta, periodic residential tenancies are governed by the Residential Tenancies Act (RSA 2000, c. R-17), supplemented by the Residential Tenancies Ministerial Regulation (Alta. Reg. 211/2004). Tenancy disputes and eviction proceedings fall under the authority of the Residential Tenancy Dispute Resolution Service (RTDRS) and the Alberta Court of Justice.

The legal mechanics governing termination of periodic month-to-month tenancies are codified in Section 6, Section 7, and Section 11 of the Act. Alberta law enforces an asymmetric notice structure based upon whether the notice is issued by the tenant or the landlord. Under Section 6, a tenant terminating a monthly periodic tenancy must provide at least one full tenancy month written notice. Conversely, a landlord seeking to terminate a periodic tenancy must provide three full tenancy months notice.

Crucially, under Alberta RTA Section 11, a landlord does not have the legal right to terminate a periodic tenancy arbitrarily or without cause. A valid landlord periodic notice must explicitly assert and establish one of the prescribed statutory grounds set out in the Ministerial Regulation. Attempting to terminate a periodic tenancy without citing and proving lawful grounds renders the notice void, and any subsequent application to the RTDRS for possession will be summarily dismissed.

Legal Service of Process: How to Deliver Notice in Alberta

Under Section 57 of the Residential Tenancies Act, notice to terminate must be served strictly through authorized channels to be enforceable in RTDRS or court proceedings.

Authorized service methods include:

  • Personal In-Hand Delivery: Handing the document directly to the recipient or an adult who appears to reside with them. Effective on the date of delivery.
  • Registered Mail: Sending by registered post. Service is complete when the postal service delivers the item or leaves notice of delivery.
  • Substituted Posting: If the tenant cannot be served personally after reasonable attempts, the landlord may post the notice in a conspicuous place upon the premises or hand it to an adult residing there.
  • Electronic Service: Permissible only where both parties have established a course of dealing using electronic transmission or written consent exists in the tenancy agreement.

Fatal Drafting Mistakes That Invalidate an Alberta Periodic Notice

1. Landlord Serving Without Prescribed Cause

Under Alberta RTA Section 11, a landlord periodic notice lacking an enumerated statutory reason is void. Tenants can challenge the notice at RTDRS and retain possession.

2. Service After the 1st of the Month Without Adding a Month

Serving notice on the 2nd of a month does not permit termination at the end of that same month. The notice cycle begins on the 1st of the subsequent month.

3. Landlord Renovation Notice Without Permits

Terminating a periodic tenancy for renovations requires that municipal building and development permits are issued prior to serving notice. Serving notice before obtaining permits is invalid.

4. Omitting the 10-Day Security Deposit Accounting Statement

Under Section 46, landlords failing to provide an itemized deduction statement within 10 days of surrender forfeit the statutory right to claim deductions for cleaning or repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much notice must an Alberta tenant give to terminate a periodic monthly tenancy?

Under Alberta Residential Tenancies Act Section 6, a tenant must give one full tenancy month notice. Notice must be served on or before the first day of the tenancy month to be effective on the last day of that tenancy month.

How much notice must an Alberta landlord give to terminate a periodic monthly tenancy?

Under Alberta Residential Tenancies Act Section 6 and Section 11, a landlord must provide at least three full tenancy months written notice. The notice must state an enumerated statutory justification and be served on or before the first day of the three-month period.

Can an Alberta landlord terminate a periodic tenancy without cause?

No. Under the Alberta Residential Tenancies Act and Ministerial Regulations, a landlord terminating a periodic tenancy must have qualifying statutory cause, such as personal occupancy by the landlord or family, purchaser occupancy, major permitted renovations, or commercial conversion.

What is the deadline for an Alberta landlord to return a security deposit?

Under Alberta Residential Tenancies Act Section 46, the landlord must return the security deposit with any accrued statutory interest or provide an itemized statement of deductions within 10 calendar days of the tenant vacating.

What happens if notice is served on the second day of the month in Alberta?

Under Alberta law, if notice is served after the first day of the month (such as on the 2nd), the full notice cycle does not begin until the first day of the following calendar month, extending the termination date by an entire month.

What tribunal resolves tenancy disputes in Alberta?

Tenancy disputes in Alberta are adjudicated by the Residential Tenancy Dispute Resolution Service (RTDRS) or the Alberta Court of Justice.
