Ontario LTB Forms Directory: Every Landlord and Tenant Board Form Explained

Ontario tenancy paperwork runs on Landlord and Tenant Board forms. N forms are notices served on the other side, L forms are landlord applications, and T forms are tenant applications. This directory explains the main forms and links to the official Tribunals Ontario copy of each.

How the Form Letters Work

The first letter of an Ontario Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) form tells you what kind of document it is. N forms are notices: a landlord or tenant fills one in and serves it on the other side. A notice starts a process, but it is not a Board order, and serving one costs nothing. L forms are landlord applications and T forms are tenant applications: these are filed with the Board itself, carry a filing fee, and lead to a hearing or an order. A forms cover questions either side can ask the Board, and C forms are used only by non-profit housing co-operatives.

In most disputes the order is fixed: serve the right N notice first, wait out its termination date, and only then file the matching L or T application if the problem is still there. Filing the wrong form, or an old version of the right one, is one of the most common reasons applications are refused or dismissed.

What changed on 21 September 2026: the Bill 60 amendments took effect and the Board reissued many forms. The N4 termination period fell from 14 days to 7 days for every type of tenancy. An N12 served for the landlord's own use no longer requires one month's rent compensation when it gives the tenant 120 days or more notice; a purchaser's own-use notice still requires it. Offence fines under the Act now reach $100,000 for an individual and $500,000 for a corporation. If you printed a form before that date, download a fresh copy instead of using the old one.

Landlord Notices: The N1, N4, N5, N12 and N13

These are the notices a landlord serves on a tenant. Each PDF link below goes to the official form on the Tribunals Ontario website and was checked when this directory was updated.

Form Official Title What It Is For Official Copy
N1 Notice of Rent Increase Tells the tenant the rent will go up on a stated date. For a rent-controlled unit the increase is given on 90 days' notice and kept within the provincial guideline. N1 PDF
N4 Notice to End your Tenancy Early for Non-payment of Rent The first step when rent is unpaid. Since 21 September 2026 the termination date can be as early as 7 days after the notice is served. Paying everything owed before that date voids the notice. N4 PDF
N5 Notice to End your Tenancy for Interfering with Others, Damage or Overcrowding Used when the tenant's conduct, damage to the unit, or too many people living in the unit is the problem. A first N5 is voidable: if the tenant corrects the behaviour in time, the notice is cancelled. N5 PDF
N12 Notice to End your Tenancy Because the Landlord, a Purchaser or a Family Member Requires the Unit The own-use notice. It needs at least 60 days and must end on the last day of a rental period or of the term. One month's rent compensation is normally owed, but not on a landlord's own-use notice that gives 120 days or more; a purchaser's notice still requires it. N12 PDF
N13 Notice to End your Tenancy Because the Landlord Wants to Demolish the Rental Unit, Repair it or Convert it to Another Use Used when the unit will be demolished, converted to a non-residential use, or repaired or renovated so extensively that it has to sit empty while the work is done. N13 PDF

Own-use notices are the most disputed forms in this group. Before serving an N12, read our full walkthrough of the grounds, the compensation rules and the bad-faith penalties in the Ontario Form N12 landlord's own-use guide.

Tenant Notices: The N9 and N15

Form Official Title What It Is For Official Copy
N9 Tenant's Notice to End the Tenancy The tenant's own notice to move out. It requires at least 60 days for a monthly or fixed-term tenancy, and the termination date must be the last day of a rental period or of the term. N9 PDF
N15 Tenant's Notice to End my Tenancy Because of Fear of Sexual or Domestic Violence and Abuse Lets a tenant end a tenancy on 28 days' notice, without waiting for the term to end, where the tenant or a child living in the unit has experienced sexual or domestic violence or abuse. It must be sent with the Board's Tenant's Statement form or a copy of a restraining order or peace bond, and the landlord is legally required to keep those documents confidential. N15 PDF

Applications and Special Forms: L1, L2, T2, T5, A1 and C1

Applications are filed with the Board rather than served on the other side. Most landlord and tenant applications can be filed online through the Tribunals Ontario Portal, which the Board says will guide you through the application step by step.

Form Official Title What It Is For Official Copy
L1 Application to Evict a Tenant for Non-payment of Rent and to Collect Rent the Tenant Owes Filed after an N4 when the tenant has neither paid everything owed nor moved out by the termination date. It asks the Board for an eviction order and an order for the rent owing. L1 PDF
L2 Application to End a Tenancy and Evict a Tenant or Collect Money The general landlord application. It is the form filed after most other termination notices, including the N5, N12 and N13, to end the tenancy, evict the tenant, or collect money owed. L2 PDF
T2 Application about Tenant Rights A tenant's application about their rights, used for problems such as the landlord entering the unit illegally, a lockout or changed locks, a withheld vital service, or harassment and substantial interference with reasonable enjoyment. T2 PDF
T5 Tenant Application: Landlord Gave a Notice of Termination in Bad Faith Filed by a tenant who has already moved out and believes an N12 or N13 notice was not given in good faith, for example because the unit was advertised or re-rented instead of being used for the stated purpose. T5 PDF
A1 Application about Whether the Act Applies Either a landlord or a tenant asks the Board to decide whether the Residential Tenancies Act covers the rental unit at all. Coverage decides which of the other forms can be used. LTB forms index
C1 Application to End the Occupancy and Evict the Member based on Non-Payment of Regular Monthly Housing Charges Only for non-profit housing co-operatives. The co-op applies to end a member's occupancy of a member unit and evict the member for unpaid regular monthly housing charges, and to collect the charges owed. LTB forms index

A note on the last two rows: the direct PDF addresses for the A1 and C1 forms currently return an error on the Board's own website, and older directories around the web still link to those dead addresses. Rather than repeat a broken link, the A1 and C1 rows above point to the Board's official forms index, where both forms are listed. Frequently Asked Questions

Where should I download official LTB forms from?

Always from Tribunals Ontario, the Board's own site. The Board revises its forms, and an out-of-date version can get a notice or application rejected. Every PDF linked on this page was checked against the Board's site. The direct PDF addresses for forms A1 and C1 currently return an error on the official site itself, so those two rows link to the Board's forms index instead of a broken file.

What is the difference between an N form and an L or T form?

An N form is a notice. One side serves it on the other, there is no filing fee, and by itself it does not produce a Board order. L forms are applications a landlord files with the Board, and T forms are applications a tenant files. Applications carry a filing fee and lead to a hearing or an order.

Which LTB form do I use when rent is unpaid?

Serve a Form N4 first. Since 21 September 2026 its termination date can be as early as 7 days after service. If the tenant has not paid everything owed or moved out by that date, the landlord files a Form L1 to ask the Board for an eviction order and an order for the rent owed.

What changed for Ontario LTB forms on 21 September 2026?

The Bill 60 amendments took effect and the Board reissued many of its forms. The N4 notice period fell from 14 days to 7 days, and an N12 served for a landlord's own use no longer requires one month's rent compensation when it gives the tenant 120 days or more notice. A purchaser's own-use N12 still requires compensation. Forms printed before the change may no longer be accepted, so download a fresh copy.
