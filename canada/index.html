Frequently Asked Questions: Canadian Tenancies

Why must an Ontario tenant notice terminate on the last day of a rental period?

Under Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 44, a tenant giving 60 days notice must ensure the termination date falls on the final day of a rental period. If rent is due on the first of the month, termination must occur on the 30th or 31st. Mid-month dates are legally void before the Landlord and Tenant Board.

What is the February exception for 60-day notices in Ontario?

Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 44(4) creates a statutory exception for February. If notice is given on or before January 1, the tenancy may terminate on the last day of February, even though fewer than 60 days elapse. Similarly, notice served on or before February 1 may terminate on March 31.

How does British Columbia calculate the effective date for a 1-month tenant notice?

Under British Columbia Residential Tenancy Act Section 45, a tenant notice must provide one full rental month and end on the day immediately preceding the date rent is due. For rent due on the first, notice given in October takes effect November 30. If mailed, five deemed service days must be added.

Can an Alberta landlord terminate a periodic tenancy without cause?

No. Under the Alberta Residential Tenancies Act, landlords cannot terminate a periodic tenancy without enumerated statutory grounds. Lawful grounds are restricted to landlord or family occupancy, purchaser occupancy, major permitted renovations, conversion to non-residential use, or demolition.

Are security deposits legal in Ontario rental housing?

No. Traditional damage or security deposits are illegal in Ontario under Residential Tenancies Act Section 105. Landlords may only collect a rent deposit equal to one rental period (Last Month Rent) and a refundable key deposit covering actual replacement cost.

How much interest must an Ontario landlord pay on Last Month Rent deposits?

Under Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 106(6), landlords must pay interest annually at the official Ontario Rent Increase Guideline percentage in effect when payment is due. For 2024, 2025, and 2026, the annual rate is 2.5 percent, which may offset lawful rent increase top-ups.
