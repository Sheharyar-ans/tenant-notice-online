Governing Legal Principles of Quebec Residential Leases

Residential tenancy in the Province of Québec is regulated by Chapter IV of Title Two of the Civil Code of Québec (CCQ Articles 1851 to 2000) and adjudicated exclusively by the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL). Unlike common law jurisdictions that rely on monthly notice to quit mechanisms, Quebec law operates on the principle of the tenant right to maintain occupancy (maintien dans les lieux). A residential lease renews automatically on identical terms unless either party serves formal written notice within strict statutory calendar horizons.

Tenant Non-Renewal Windows Under CCQ Article 1942

Under Article 1942 of the Civil Code of Québec, a lessee who elects not to renew their lease must provide written notification to the lessor within precise statutory boundaries:

  • Fixed Lease of 12 Months or Longer: The tenant must provide notice between 3 and 6 months prior to the lease expiration date. For the traditional Quebec lease term running July 1 to June 30, the non-renewal notice must be received by the landlord no earlier than January 1 and no later than March 31.
  • Fixed Lease of Less than 12 Months: The tenant must deliver notice between 1 and 2 months before the lease expiry date.
  • Indeterminate or Periodic Tenancy: Notice must be given between 1 and 2 months prior to the intended surrender date.

Failure to deliver notice within the statutory window results in the automatic renewal (reconduction tacite) of the lease for an identical period, legally obligating the tenant for another year of rental payments unless a lease transfer is executed.

Landlord Repossession and Eviction Standards

A housing provider in Quebec cannot terminate a lease without cause. Repossession of a dwelling (reprise de logement) under Article 1957 of the Civil Code of Québec is strictly restricted to designated personal beneficiaries:

  • The lessor themselves.
  • The lessor's parents or children.
  • A spouse from whom the lessor is separated or divorced, or an in-law, provided the lessor is their main support.

Under Article 1960 of the CCQ, the landlord must provide at least 6 months advance notice before the lease expiry for a 12-month lease (or 1 month for leases of 6 months or less). The notice must explicitly state the intended date of repossession, the full name of the beneficiary, their relationship to the lessor, and the conditions governing the repossession.

Senior Tenant Immunity Under Bill 65 (CCQ Article 1959.1)

Quebec law establishes strong protections for vulnerable senior citizens. Under Article 1959.1 of the CCQ, strengthened by the passage of Bill 65, a landlord is legally prohibited from repossessing a dwelling or evicting a tenant if the tenant (or their spouse) meets three cumulative statutory tests: they are 70 years of age or older; they have resided in the rental dwelling for at least 10 years; and their household income is equal to or below the maximum qualifying limit for low-rental social housing. Any repossession notice served against a qualifying protected senior is void by operation of law.

Bill 31 Lease Assignment Reforms (Cession de bail)

Enacted under Bill 31, substantial modifications were made to Article 1870 of the Civil Code of Québec governing lease assignments. Previously, tenants possessed the ability to assign a lease to an eligible replacement candidate, and landlords could only refuse on serious grounds (such as credit insolvency). Under current statutory rules, if a landlord refuses an assignment candidate for reasons other than a serious default, the lease is extinguished on the date the assignment would have taken effect. This releases the departing tenant from future rental liabilities without penalty, ending the multi-decade practice of forced lease transfers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the legal deadline for a tenant to give notice of non-renewal in Quebec? ▼

Under Article 1942 of the Civil Code of Québec, for a residential lease of 12 months or more, the tenant must provide written notice of non-renewal between 3 and 6 months before the lease expiration date. For a standard July 1 to June 30 lease, the notice must be received by the landlord between January 1 and March 31.

Can a Quebec landlord refuse a lease assignment under Bill 31? ▼

Under recent legislative reforms under Bill 31 amending the Civil Code of Québec, if a landlord refuses an assignment for a non-serious reason, the lease is terminated as of the requested assignment date. The tenant is released from contractual liability without financial penalty, although the landlord is no longer forced to accept the assignee.

How much advance notice is required for repossession of a dwelling by the owner? ▼

Under Article 1960 of the Civil Code of Québec, a landlord must provide at least 6 months notice before the end of the term for a lease of 12 months or longer. For a lease ending June 30, the repossession notice must be served on or before December 31.

What protections exist for senior tenants against repossession or eviction in Quebec? ▼

Under Article 1959.1 of the Civil Code of Québec, as strengthened by Bill 65, a landlord cannot repossess or evict a tenant who is 70 years of age or older, has occupied the dwelling for at least 10 years, and has income equal to or less than the maximum qualifying threshold for low-rental housing, unless the landlord also meets specific age criteria.

What is the procedure for unpaid rent under Article 1971 of the Civil Code of Québec? ▼

Under Article 1971 of the Civil Code of Québec, a landlord may petition the Tribunal administratif du logement for resiliation of the lease when the tenant is more than three weeks (21 days) late in paying rent, or when frequent late payments cause serious prejudice to the lessor.
