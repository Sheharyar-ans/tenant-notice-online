Statutory Legal Foundation & Governing Act

Under the Ontario Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (S.O. 2006, c. 17), the financial framework governing residential security deposits differs sharply from the United States and other Canadian jurisdictions. Pursuant to Section 105 of the Act, traditional damage deposits, cleaning deposits, and pet deposits are completely illegal. A landlord is statutorily prohibited from requiring or collecting any deposit from a prospective or current tenant other than a rent deposit for the last rental period of the tenancy (commonly referred to as Last Month's Rent or LMR) and a reasonable refundable key deposit limited to the actual direct replacement cost.

Because the landlord holds the tenant's Last Month Rent deposit throughout the entire duration of the tenancy, Section 106(6) of the Act establishes a mandatory affirmative obligation on the part of the landlord: the landlord shall pay interest to the tenant annually on the amount of the deposit. The statutory rate of interest is not arbitrary; it is strictly defined as equal to the Ontario Rent Increase Guideline determined under Section 120 that is in effect at the time payment becomes due.

Crucially, Section 106(7) sets out the statutory offsetting mechanism. The landlord may apply the interest owing under subsection (6) to the amount of the deposit so that the amount of the deposit remains equal to the current lawful rent. When a landlord serves a compliant Notice of Rent Increase (Form N1) raising rent by the exact annual guideline percentage, the statutory deposit top-up required from the tenant under Section 106(3) is identical in dollar value to the interest owed by the landlord under Section 106(6). If the landlord applies the interest to the deposit, both obligations neutralize each other completely. However, if the landlord never raised the rent, or raised the rent below the guideline, the tenant remains entitled to direct payment of the accrued interest.

How Deposit Interest Offsets Annual Rent Increases

A frequent source of dispute between Ontario landlords and tenants is whether interest must be paid directly by cheque or e-transfer each year. Section 106(7) provides landlords with the statutory discretion to credit the interest toward the deposit balance rather than issuing cash payment.

Consider a concrete scenario: A tenant signs a lease on November 1, 2023, paying $2,000 for Last Month's Rent. On November 1, 2024, the landlord issues a valid Form N1 rent increase raising rent by the 2.5% statutory guideline to $2,050. Under Section 106(3), the tenant is obligated to top up the rent deposit by $50 so it matches the new monthly rent. Simultaneously, under Section 106(6), the landlord owes the tenant 2.5% interest on the $2,000 deposit, which equals exactly $50. By applying Section 106(7), the $50 interest is rolled into the deposit, bringing the deposit held to $2,050 without any out-of-pocket payment required from either party.

Conversely, if the landlord fails to issue an annual rent increase, the monthly rent remains $2,000. In this case, no deposit top-up is owed. The landlord cannot retain the $50 interest; it must be paid directly to the tenant or deducted from the next rent installment.

Remedies for Unpaid Deposit Interest in Ontario

If a landlord fails to pay statutory interest or properly credit it to the deposit, Ontario tenants possess explicit legal remedies:

  • Direct Rent Deduction: Section 106(6) explicitly confirms that the tenant may deduct the owed interest from a subsequent rent payment. It is prudent practice to deliver this written statement and ledger to the landlord prior to making the deduction.
  • LTB Form T1 Application: The tenant may file a Form T1 (Tenant Application for a Rebate of Money the Landlord Owes) with the Landlord and Tenant Board under Section 135. The Board will issue an order requiring the landlord to pay the interest plus reimburse the tenant's board filing fee.
  • One-Year Limitation Period: Under Section 135.1 of the RTA, applications for interest rebates must be filed within one year of the date the interest payment became due. Delaying action beyond one year can extinguish the statutory right to recover through the board.

Fatal Legal Mistakes in Ontario Deposit Management

1. Demanding Security or Damage Deposits

Collecting separate damage deposits violates Section 105. Tenants can file Form T1 immediately to demand full refund of illegal deposit funds with interest.

2. Demanding Out-of-Pocket Top-Ups Without Crediting Interest

Landlords who demand cash top-ups from tenants after a rent increase without crediting the accrued statutory interest are collecting excess rent in violation of the Act.

3. Missing the One-Year LTB Limitation Period

Tenants who wait multiple years to address unpaid interest risk losing claims older than twelve months due to Section 135.1 statutory limitation bars.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are security deposits or damage deposits legal in Ontario?

No. Under Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 105, traditional security deposits, pet damage deposits, and cleaning deposits are strictly illegal. A landlord may only collect a rent deposit for the last rental period (Last Month Rent) and a refundable key deposit.

What is the interest rate an Ontario landlord must pay on Last Month Rent?

Under Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 106(6), the landlord must pay interest annually at the official Ontario Rent Increase Guideline percentage in effect when payment is due. For 2024, 2025, and 2026, the annual rate is 2.5 percent.

How does LMR interest offset an annual rent increase?

Under Section 106(7), the landlord may apply the interest owing directly to the deposit. Because the rent increase guideline percentage exactly matches the interest rate, when a landlord raises rent by the guideline, the interest earned offsets the tenant deposit top-up, leaving zero net interest payable.

What happens if the landlord does not increase the rent?

If the landlord does not increase the rent by the guideline percentage, no top-up is required from the tenant. The landlord must pay the entire interest amount directly to the tenant annually or allow the tenant to deduct it from their rent.

Can an Ontario tenant deduct unpaid deposit interest from their monthly rent?

Yes. Under Section 106(6) of the Act, if the landlord fails to pay interest when due, the tenant is statutorily entitled to deduct the owed interest amount from a subsequent rent payment or file a Form T1 application with the Landlord and Tenant Board.

What is the limitation period to claim unpaid LMR interest at the LTB?

Under Section 135.1 of the Ontario Residential Tenancies Act, a tenant has one year from the date the interest payment became due to file a Form T1 application with the Landlord and Tenant Board to recover unpaid interest.
