Statutory Legal Foundation & Governing Act

In the province of Ontario, residential tenancies are governed strictly by the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (S.O. 2006, c. 17). When a tenant decides to vacate a residential unit under a monthly periodic tenancy, yearly tenancy, or at the conclusion of a fixed term lease, statutory compliance hinges upon the proper execution of Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) Form N9, titled Tenant's Notice to End the Tenancy. Under Section 44 of the Act, a tenant must give sixty calendar days advance written notice.

Crucially, Section 44(1) imposes a mandatory statutory constraint known as the rental period alignment rule. The effective termination date selected by the tenant cannot simply be any arbitrary date sixty days in the future. The date must coincide with the final day of a rental period. For the vast majority of Ontario tenancies where rent is payable on the first calendar day of each month, the rental period concludes on the thirtieth or thirty-first of the month (or twenty-eighth/twenty-ninth of February). Setting a termination date mid-month, such as the fifteenth, renders the notice defective on its face and void before the Landlord and Tenant Board.

Tenants residing under fixed-term tenancy agreements (commonly twelve-month leases) are subject to Section 44(3). The earliest permissible termination date on Form N9 cannot precede the expiration date of the fixed term. If a lease began on September 1 and ends on August 31 of the following year, the tenant must deliver Form N9 at least sixty days prior to August 31, designating August 31 as the statutory end date. Once notice is served, the tenancy dissolves on that date without automatically converting into a statutory month-to-month tenancy under Section 38.

Legal Service of Process: How to Deliver Form N9 in Ontario

Under Section 191 of the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 and Landlord and Tenant Board Rules of Practice Rule 3, legal notices must be served using an approved statutory delivery method. Failure to establish proof of service is a primary reason notices are dismissed during board hearings.

Acceptable methods of service include:

  • Handing in person: Delivering the document directly to the landlord, superintendent, or property manager. Service takes effect on the exact day of delivery.
  • Leaving in mailbox: Placing the notice in the landlord's mailbox or where mail is customarily deposited. Service takes effect on the day of placement.
  • Courier delivery: Sending via commercial courier service. Notice is legally deemed received on the day after courier delivery.
  • Regular or registered mail: Under LTB Rules, mail service is deemed delivered on the fifth calendar day after mailing. A tenant mailing notice must add five days to the sixty-day calculation.
  • Electronic transmission (Email): Permissible only if the written residential tenancy agreement contains an express consent clause for service by email. Text messaging does not satisfy statutory requirements.

Fatal Drafting Mistakes That Invalidate an Ontario Form N9

1. Selecting a Mid-Month Termination Date

Specifying a termination date such as the 15th when rent is payable on the 1st violates Section 44(1). The Landlord and Tenant Board will dismiss the notice as legally defective.

2. Failing to Account for Mail Delivery Delay

Under LTB Rule 3, mailed notices add five calendar days. A notice mailed on November 1 for a December 31 termination is legally deemed served on November 6, providing only 55 days notice and invalidating the termination.

3. Premature Fixed-Term Termination

Attempting to terminate prior to the end date of a fixed term lease without mutual consent (Form N11) or statutory assignment refusal (s. 95) is invalid under Section 44(3).

4. Omitting Signatures of All Named Tenants

Where multiple tenants are named on the tenancy agreement, all signatories should execute the notice to ensure complete surrender of leasehold rights and avoid joint liability disputes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days notice must an Ontario tenant give on Form N9?

Under Ontario Residential Tenancies Act Section 44, a tenant in a monthly periodic tenancy or fixed term tenancy must provide at least 60 full calendar days notice before the effective termination date.

Why must the termination date fall on the last day of a rental period?

Section 44(1) of the Ontario Residential Tenancies Act explicitly dictates that notice must be effective on the last day of a rental period. If rent is payable on the first of each month, the notice must terminate on the final calendar day of the month. Setting a mid-month date invalidates the notice before the Landlord and Tenant Board.

How does the February exception work under Ontario RTA Section 44(4)?

Under Ontario RTA Section 44(4), if notice is served on or before January 1, the tenant may terminate the tenancy on the last day of February, even though February has only 28 or 29 days. If notice is given on or before February 1, the termination date may be March 31.

What happens if an Ontario Form N9 is sent by regular or registered mail?

Under Ontario Landlord and Tenant Board Rules and RTA Section 191, notices sent by regular or registered mail are deemed to be received five calendar days after mailing. Tenants must add five days to their 60-day count to avoid short service.

Can a tenant cancel an Ontario Form N9 once delivered to the landlord?

No. Once a tenant serves Form N9, it is legally binding upon both parties. Under Section 77 of the RTA, a landlord can apply immediately to the Landlord and Tenant Board for an ex parte eviction order (Form L3) without a hearing if the tenant fails to vacate.

Does an Ontario tenant have to pay rent for the final 60 days?

The tenant pays regular rent for the first 30 days of the notice period. For the final 30 days, the landlord must apply the Last Month Rent deposit held from the start of the tenancy pursuant to RTA Section 106.
