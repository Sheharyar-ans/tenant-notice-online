Statutory Foundations of Manitoba Tenancy Law

Residential rental tenancies in Manitoba are governed by The Residential Tenancies Act (C.C.S.M. c. R119) and regulated through the Residential Tenancies Branch (RTB). The legislation provides comprehensive rights for both tenants and housing providers, enforcing standardized procedures for periodic lease termination, non-payment of rent, landlord personal occupancy repossessions, and dispute arbitration.

Tenant Periodic Termination Rules Under Section 86

Under Section 86 of The Residential Tenancies Act, a tenant who rents under a periodic monthly tenancy may terminate the agreement by serving at least one full rental payment period written notice. In practice, because rental payment periods run from the first day to the final day of a calendar month, the notice must be delivered to the landlord on or before the last day of the prior month:

  • End-of-Month Delivery Requirement: To terminate a tenancy effective May 31, the written notice must be served on or before April 30.
  • Mid-Month Default: If a tenant serves notice on May 2, the notice does not take effect on May 31. By operation of law, the one-month notice period only begins on June 1, making the statutory termination date June 30.

Section 98 Personal Occupancy and School-Age Children Protections

When a landlord requires a rental unit for personal occupancy, family use, or extensive renovations, Section 98 of the Act establishes detailed notice requirements based on municipal rental vacancy rates:

  • Standard Notice Horizon: Landlords must provide between 3 and 6 months notice depending on whether the municipal vacancy rate is above or below 2 percent.
  • Moving Expense Indemnity: Under Section 99, landlords terminating tenancies for personal occupancy or renovations must compensate the tenant for reasonable moving expenses up to $500 or statutory limits.

The School-Age Children Immunity (Section 98(3))

One of the most powerful tenant protections in Canadian law is codified in Section 98(3) of The Residential Tenancies Act. If a tenant has a child who attends a school accessible from the rental unit, any notice of termination for personal occupancy, purchaser use, or major renovations cannot take effect until the end of the school year on June 30. Even if a landlord serves a five-month notice in October, the tenant retains the absolute statutory right to remain in occupancy until June 30, preventing mid-year educational disruptions for children.

Manitoba Form 10 Non-Payment Notice Standards (Section 95)

When a tenant fails to pay rent on the agreed date, a landlord cannot immediately issue an eviction notice. Under Section 95, rent must be overdue for at least three full calendar days. On the fourth day of arrears, the landlord may serve official Form 10 (Notice of Termination by Landlord for Non-Payment of Rent), giving the tenant at least three days notice to either pay the full outstanding balance or surrender possession. If the tenant tenders the overdue rent prior to the termination date, the default is cured and the notice is cancelled.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much notice must a tenant give to terminate a month-to-month lease in Manitoba? ▼

Under Section 86 of The Residential Tenancies Act of Manitoba, a tenant in a periodic month-to-month tenancy must provide at least one full rental payment period of advance written notice. To end a tenancy on the last day of a month, the notice must be served on or before the last day of the preceding month.

What is the Manitoba school-age child protection under Section 98(3)? ▼

Under Section 98(3) of The Residential Tenancies Act, if a tenant has a child attending a school that is accessible from the rental unit, a landlord notice of termination for personal occupancy, renovations, or demolition cannot take effect until the end of the school year on June 30, regardless of when notice was served.

When can a Manitoba landlord issue Form 10 for unpaid rent? ▼

Under Section 95 of The Residential Tenancies Act, when rent remains unpaid for three or more days after the statutory due date, the landlord may serve official Form 10, giving the tenant at least three days notice to pay the full overdue balance or vacate the rental premises.

How much notice is required for landlord personal occupancy in Manitoba? ▼

Under Section 98, a landlord repossessing a rental unit for personal occupancy or family use must give between 3 and 6 months notice depending on the published rental vacancy rate in the municipality. If the local vacancy rate is below 2 percent, a minimum of 5 or 6 months notice is legally required.

What happens if a notice is served by mail in Manitoba? ▼

Under Section 184 of The Residential Tenancies Act, any notice served by ordinary or registered mail is deemed to have been received on the third day after mailing, excluding statutory holidays. Parties serving notices by mail must incorporate this three-day buffer to ensure valid notice.
