Statutory Legal Foundation & Periodic Tenancy Exit Rules

Under the modernized framework of English housing law established by the Renters' Rights Act, fixed-term assured shorthold tenancies have been replaced with a universal system of periodic rolling tenancies. In this structure, residential tenants are endowed with the statutory right to bring their tenancy to an end at any time by giving their landlord two calendar months written notice.

Under the common law principles reaffirmed by the courts in cases such as Crate v Miller and Fernandez v McDonald, a valid notice to quit served by a periodic tenant must satisfy two strict criteria:

  1. Length of Notice: It must provide at least two clear calendar months between the date of service and the intended termination date.
  2. Expiry on a Rental Period Boundary: The notice must expire on the final day of a rental period, or alternatively on the first day of the subsequent rental period. For example, if rent is payable on the 1st day of each month, the monthly rental period runs from the 1st to the final day of that month. A valid notice must therefore expire on the final day of a month (such as December 31st).

Joint Tenancies: The Unilateral Termination Rule

One of the most consequential aspects of English tenancy law concerns joint tenants residing under a periodic contract. Under the landmark House of Lords ruling in Hammersmith and Fulham LBC v Monk [1992], any one joint tenant has the legal power to serve a notice to quit on the landlord without the consent, agreement, or prior knowledge of the other joint tenants.

Once that notice expires, the periodic tenancy terminates for all tenants residing in the property as a matter of law. The remaining occupants do not retain automatic rights of continued occupation unless the landlord chooses to offer them a brand-new individual tenancy agreement.

Tenant Fees Act 2019 Protections upon Exit

When serving a notice to quit to conclude a periodic tenancy, tenants are protected by the Tenant Fees Act 2019. Landlords and letting agents are legally prohibited from levying:

  • Exit fees or surrender administration charges for processing a valid notice.
  • Compulsory professional cleaning fees unless the tenant has breached their statutory duty of returning the property to the standard established in the check-in inventory.
  • Check-out inventory fees or inspection charges.

Common Pitfalls That Void a Tenant Notice to Quit

  • Insufficient Notice Length: Providing 59 days instead of two full calendar months renders the notice void on its face, leaving the tenant legally liable for rent during subsequent periods.
  • Mid-Period Termination Dates: Demanding that the tenancy terminate on the 15th day of a calendar month when the rental period runs from the 1st to the end of the month violates common law rules and allows the landlord to reject the notice.
  • Oral or Informal Notice: Sending a text message or verbal notice over the phone is legally invalid. Tenancy agreements require formal written notice served in accordance with the agreement's service clause.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much notice must a tenant give to end a periodic tenancy in England?

Under the Renters' Rights Act, tenants on statutory periodic tenancies have the legal right to terminate their tenancy by serving at least 2 calendar months written notice to quit on their landlord or letting agent.

Does a tenant notice to quit need to end on a specific day of the rental period?

Yes. Under common law rules governing periodic tenancies, a tenant's notice to quit must expire on either the first or last day of a rental period (typically the day preceding the date monthly rent falls due).

Can a tenant withdraw a notice to quit once served?

No. A valid notice to quit served by a tenant brings the tenancy to an end upon expiry and cannot be withdrawn unilaterally. It can only be rescinded if the landlord expressly agrees in writing to grant a new tenancy.

Can a landlord charge fees for a tenant ending a periodic tenancy?

No. Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019, landlords and letting agents are prohibited from charging check-out fees, administrative termination fees, or inventory exit fees to tenants terminating a periodic tenancy.

What happens if one joint tenant serves a notice to quit?

Under established English property law, a valid notice to quit served by one joint tenant on a periodic tenancy terminates the tenancy for all joint tenants, regardless of whether the other joint tenants consented.
