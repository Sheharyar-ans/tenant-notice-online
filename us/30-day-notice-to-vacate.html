California 30-Day Notice to Vacate Generator: Civ. Code § 1946.1

Direct Legal Rule: California Civil Code Section 1946.1 establishes that residential landlords or tenants terminating a month-to-month tenancy must provide thirty days written notice if tenancy is under one year, or sixty days if over one year. Texas, Florida, and New York enforce parallel statutory rules under state property codes.

Statutory Legal Foundation & Governing State Civil Codes

Terminating a periodic residential tenancy in the United States requires strict adherence to state legislative enactments. In California, notice requirements are governed by California Civil Code Section 1946.1. Under this statute, an owner of a residential dwelling giving notice to terminate a periodic tenancy must provide at least 30 days notice if any tenant or resident has resided in the dwelling for less than one year. If all tenants and residents have resided continuously in the dwelling for one year or more, the mandatory statutory notice window expands to not less than 60 days.

Additionally, practitioners must account for the California Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (Civil Code Section 1946.2, commonly enacted via Assembly Bill 1482). Where a residential tenancy is not exempt, a landlord cannot issue a notice to vacate without asserting valid at-fault just cause (such as nonpayment of rent or material breach of covenant) or no-fault just cause (such as substantial remodeling, owner move-in, or withdrawal from the rental market under the Ellis Act). For no-fault just cause terminations, landlords are mandated to furnish direct tenant relocation assistance equal to one month of rent within 15 calendar days of service.

In Texas, periodic lease termination is governed by Texas Property Code Section 91.001. A monthly tenancy may be terminated by the tenant or the landlord giving notice to the other. The notice must specify the termination date and must be given at least 30 days before the day on which the tenancy is to terminate, unless the parties agreed in writing to a different period. In Florida, Florida Statutes Section 83.57 dictates that month-to-month tenancies are terminated by giving not less than 30 days written notice prior to the end of any monthly period.

Service of Process: How to Legally Deliver This Notice

Drafting a compliant notice to vacate is ineffective if service of process fails to satisfy statutory prerequisites. In judicial eviction proceedings (unlawful detainer lawsuits), defective service is an affirmative defense that strips the court of jurisdiction and results in mandatory dismissal with an award of court costs to the tenant.

1. Personal Delivery

Handing the physical document directly to the named tenant or occupants. This is the gold standard of service and provides absolute proof of actual notice.

2. Substituted Service

Leaving a copy with a competent person of suitable age and discretion at the premises, followed by mailing a second duplicate copy via first-class mail on the same business day.

3. Posting & Mailing

Affixing the notice conspicuously to the primary entrance and mailing a duplicate copy by certified mail where permitted after diligent attempts at personal service have failed.

Common Pitfalls That Void This Notice

  • Day Counting Errors: The day of physical service does not count toward the 30-day or 60-day window. Day 1 begins on the calendar day immediately following lawful service.
  • Accepting Rent After Expiration: Accepting full or partial rent for any period following the effective vacate date re-establishes the periodic tenancy as a matter of law, completely waiving the notice.
  • Retaliatory Eviction Presumptions: In California under Civil Code Section 1942.5, serving a notice within 180 days of a tenant exercising protected legal rights (such as reporting habitability defects) creates a legal presumption of retaliation.
  • Ignoring Just Cause Requirements: Serving a 30-day notice on a covered California tenant without satisfying AB 1482 just cause rules renders the notice void on its face.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statutory notice period to terminate a periodic tenancy in California?

Under California Civil Code Section 1946.1, a landlord must provide 30 days written notice if the tenant has resided in the dwelling for less than one year, or 60 days written notice if the tenant has resided there for one year or more.

Does the California Tenant Protection Act require just cause for a 30-day notice?

Yes. Under Civil Code Section 1946.2 (AB 1482), tenancies subject to statewide rent control require enumerated at-fault or no-fault just cause once any adult tenant has continuously occupied the residential property for twelve months.

How does Texas law calculate the notice period to terminate a month-to-month lease?

Texas Property Code Section 91.001 provides that a monthly tenancy may be terminated by giving at least 30 days notice before the intended termination date, unless the written lease agreement expressly stipulates a different notice period.

What are the notice tiers for periodic tenancy termination in New York?

New York Real Property Law Section 226-c mandates 30 days notice for tenancies under one year, 60 days notice for tenancies between one and two years, and 90 days notice for tenancies extending beyond two years.

Can a 30-day notice to vacate be delivered via email or text message?

No. Electronic delivery does not satisfy statutory service requirements in California, Texas, Florida, or New York. The notice must be served via personal delivery, substituted service, or certified mailing as codified by state civil procedure rules.
