UK Renters' Rights Act: The Definitive Guide to Section 21 Abolition and Reformed Tenancies

Direct Statutory Rule: The Renters' Rights Act completely abolishes Section 21 no-fault evictions across England and converts all assured shorthold tenancies into rolling periodic tenancies. Landlords seeking possession must now prove specific reformed grounds under Section 8 of the Housing Act 1988.

1. The End of Section 21 No-Fault Evictions

For decades, Section 21 of the Housing Act 1988 allowed private landlords to repossess residential properties by providing two months of written notice without assigning any fault or judicial reason. The Renters' Rights Act permanently terminates this mechanism. Any Section 21 notice served after the legislative implementation date is void and unexecutable in the County Court.

2. Modernized Section 8 Possession Grounds

With Section 21 eliminated, landlords must rely exclusively on reformed Schedule 2 grounds through the statutory Section 8 Notice to Quit. The primary grounds include:

  • Mandatory Ground 8 (Serious Rent Arrears): The mandatory threshold has been raised from 2 months to at least 3 months of unpaid rent. The notice period is set at 4 weeks. Both at the time of notice service and at the court hearing date, the tenant must be at least 3 months in arrears.
  • Ground 1A (Sale of Property): Where a private landlord legitimately intends to sell the property on the open market, they must provide at least 4 months of statutory notice. This ground cannot be utilized during the protected initial tenancy period.
  • Ground 1 (Landlord or Family Moving In): Requires a mandatory 4-month notice window and cannot be served during the first 12 months of occupation.

3. Universal Periodic Tenancies & Tenant Rights

All fixed-term assured shorthold tenancies are replaced with periodic tenancies. Tenants retain the legal right to terminate their tenancy at any point by serving a statutory 2-month written notice using the Tenant Notice to Quit Generator. The notice must expire on the final day of a rental period.

4. Challenging Above-Market Rent Increases

Rent review clauses in tenancy agreements are prohibited. Landlords can only seek to increase rent once every 52 weeks using statutory Form 4 under Section 13 of the Housing Act 1988, providing at least 2 months advance notice.

If the proposed rent exceeds genuine open market values, tenants can lodge a challenge using our Section 13 Rent Challenge Tool. The application refers the notice to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber), which conducts an objective market valuation. The tenant does not pay the increased amount until the Tribunal delivers its determination.

5. Tenancy Deposit Protection Enforcement

Statutory deposit protection under the Housing Act 2004 remains strictly enforced. Landlords who fail to register deposits within 30 days face mandatory 1x to 3x compensation penalties, actionable via our Tenancy Deposit Repayment Demand Generator.
